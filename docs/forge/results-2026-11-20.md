- Half-life convention (like-for-like with the lock): **P(ratio < 0.01) = 0.79**, median **0.0079**, 90% interval **0.0050–0.0127**. Sensitivity sweeps give P from **0.65 to 0.92**.

- The card wording now states the probability, says the “upkeep” reading is suggested rather than shown, and lists the fragilities (species not fixed in the lock; Fig. 1B growth rate; the other 122 °C point; dilute solution).

## Zipf–Gibrat — split of `x.hist.zipf-gibrat`

The single atlas record `x.hist.zipf-gibrat` (`atlas/crosslinks/history__physics__hist-zipf-gibrat.yaml`, label **MAINSTREAM-HYPOTHESIS**, status contested) is replaced by two records. Philosopher's example record `x.zipf-gibrat` in `examples/` is unchanged.

### (a) `x.math.gibrat-floor-zipf` — the theorem

**ESTABLISHED**, support=mathematics, claim=pattern, evidence rung 0 (no claim about a real system), `model_exists` true. Gabaix 1999, Proposition 1: proportional growth plus a floor gives a Zipf exponent ζ = 1/(1 − S_min/S_bar). Gibrat's law alone, with no floor, has no steady state (sizes go lognormal with growing variance). The DOI 10.1162/003355399556133 was re-checked against Crossref this session: *The Quarterly Journal of Economics* 114(3):739–767 (1999), “Zipf's Law for Cities: An Explanation”, X. Gabaix. Bibliographic record confirmed; the typeset page for Proposition 1 was not re-opened here.

This is a theorem about the model. It says nothing about whether real cities follow it.

### (b) `x.hist.gibrat-explains-zipf` — the historical claim

**CONJECTURE**, status **contested**, evidence rung 1. This is the claim that the model is the actual mechanism in real countries. It is downgraded from **MAINSTREAM-HYPOTHESIS**. `breaks_if` now calls for a pre-registered equivalence test.

Forge Claim E (lock e3bc861, WUP 2025 F21) was **KILLED** 17 vs threshold 16 under its own lock, using “CI contains the null” as “holds”. That locked verdict stands, with the caveat that it treats non-rejection as acceptance. This reanalysis does not replace it.

Post-hoc bootstrap TOST (`docs/analysis/zipf_tost.py`, 2,000 resamples, 90% percentile CI; Zipf margin [0.9, 1.1], Gibrat margin |b| ≤ 0.01, same sample rules and Hill estimator as the forge run):

- Zipf: **3 pass (EQUIV), 7 fail (NONEQUIV), 64 inconclusive** (N_Z = 74). The three equivalent countries are CHN, IND and RUS.
- Gibrat (|b| ≤ 0.01): 0 EQUIV, 14 NONEQUIV, 50 INCONCL (N_G = 64).
- Claim E has **one decisive country: China, against** (Zipf equivalent, Gibrat clearly not: b = +0.051, big cities growing faster).
- Passing a ±0.1 Zipf margin needs roughly **270+ cities** per country (Hill 90% interval half-width ≈ 1.645 α / √n). Most countries have fewer, so country-level equivalence is structurally out of reach.
- Robustness at a 100k cutoff: N_Z 51 (0 EQUIV, 7 NONEQUIV, 44 INCONCL); no decisive E units.

**Caveats.** UN WUP figures are DEGURBA model estimates, not census counts (already flagged in the forge run). The Gibrat equivalence margin was Mathematician's choice; the same run also reports |b| ≤ 0.005 and |b| ≤ 0.02. At ±0.01 and ±0.005, China remains the only decisive E unit and still goes against; at ±0.02 two countries are both-equivalent, and China is still against.
